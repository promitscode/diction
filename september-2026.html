Monthly Archive

September 2026

A continuing archive of words, meanings, and sentences gathered through September.

September 1

New Word: Careened = (V.) Moved rapidly and uncontrollably, often swerving violently from side to side or from one direction to another. It conveys three things at once: speed + instability + lack of control.

New Sentence: The negotiations careened from optimism to acrimony within a matter of days.

September 2

New Word: Capricious = (Adj.) changing suddenly and unpredictably, often for no clear or rational reason.

New Sentence: He was a capricious boss, generous one moment and vindictive the next.

September 3

New Word: Vestigial = (Adj.) What remains is rudimentary, diminished and often barely functional, with an echo of its former importance.

New Sentence: After successive rounds of tariffs, only vestigial traces of the old free-trading relationship remained.

September 4

New Word: Slapstick = (Adj.) A style of broad, exaggerated physical comedy involving falls, collisions, blows, clumsiness, chases and other absurd physical mishaps.

New Sentence: His performance has a slapstick quality, relying more on physical mishaps than verbal wit.

September 7

New Word: Mortified = (Adj.) Extremely embarrassed, humiliated, or ashamed.

New Sentence: He looked mortified after confidently giving the wrong answer in front of the entire room.

September 8

New Word: Basket case = (N.) A basket case is a person, organisation, economy, system, etc. that is in an extremely bad state and seemingly unable to function properly.

New Sentence: I thought I'd be a basket case before the speech, but I was surprisingly calm.

September 9

New Word: Glutted = (Adj.) filled or supplied with far more of something than is needed or can be absorbed.

New Sentence: In schlepped my glutted tote bag acros town.

September 10

New Word: Bashful = (Adj.) shy, self-conscious, or easily embarrassed, especially in social situations.

New Sentence: She was surprisingly bashful about discussing her own achievements.

September 11

New Word: Plaintive = (Adj.) sounding sad, mournful or wistful, often in a way that quietly expresses longing, sorrow or an appeal for sympathy.

New Sentence: The film is an elegy for a dying rural community. Its most poignant moment comes when an old farmer leaves his home for the last time. His daughter's plaintive goodbye gives the scene its extraordinary pathos.

September 14

New Word: Venerable = (Adj.) deserving great respect because of age, history, wisdom, character or long-standing importance.

New Sentence: The venerable actor received a standing ovation as he walked onto the stage.

September 15

New Word: Detritus = (N.) the debris, fragments or discarded material left behind after something has been used, damaged, destroyed or has decayed.

New Sentence: The abandoned factories were the detritus of the city's industrial past.

September 16

New Word: Paramours = (N.) a lover, particularly a secret or illicit lover.

New Sentence: The novel follows a wealthy aristocrat and the various paramours who drift through his life.

September 17

New Word: Auteur = (N.) A filmmaker—usually a director—whose films bear such a distinctive personal vision and style that they can be regarded as the “author” of those films.

New Sentence: The director has emerged as one of contemporary cinema's most distinctive auteurs.

September 18

New Word: Import = (N.) importance, significance, or weight. Can be used in place of 'importance'

New Sentence: Ideas gained import simply because he had articulated them.

September 21

New Word: Desultory = (Adj.) Lacking a clear plan, purpose, or consistency; moving from one thing to another in a disconnected or half-hearted way.

New Sentence: After dinner, they fell into a desultory conversation about books, politics and old friends.

September 22

New Word: Philistinism = (N.) A lack of interest in, understanding of, or appreciation for art, culture, literature, or intellectual pursuits.

New Sentence: His contempt for poetry and painting was less ignorance than a kind of deliberate philistinism.

September 23

New Word: Smorgasbord = (N.) Originally, a Swedish buffet offering a wide variety of dishes. Figuratively, it means a large and varied assortment of things. It is more vivid than array or range, but also less restrained.

New Sentence: The festival offered a smorgasbord of films from more than 40 countries.

September 24

New Word: Polemical = (Adj.) Usually suggests writing or speech that is combative. It is not merely “critical”; it implies a willingness to argue forcefully and often to provoke disagreement.

New Sentence: The speech was more polemical than conciliatory, leaving little room for compromise.

September 25

New Word: Disparaging = (Adj.) Disparaging is stronger than merely critical. It suggests that the speaker is putting someone or something down, often with contempt, scorn, or condescension.

New Sentence: He made disparaging remarks about the company’s competitors.

September 28

New Word: Haggling = (N.) arguing or negotiating back and forth over a price or the precise terms of a deal, with each side trying to secure better terms.

New Sentence: The two governments spent weeks haggling over the details of the agreement.

September 29

New Word: Mollified = (V.) made someone less angry, anxious, hostile, or dissatisfied. Soothe a person's displeasure.

New Sentence: Investors were mollified by the central bank's assurance that it stood ready to intervene.
