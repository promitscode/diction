Monthly Archive

August 2026

A continuing archive of words, meanings, and sentences gathered through August.

August 3

New Word: Doozy = (N.) Highly informal. Something remarkable, extraordinary, or extreme of its kind. Coul dbe used in good or bad terms.

New Sentence: That was a doozy of a party. / The next question was a doozy.

August 4

New Word: Ho-hum = (Adj.) boring, ordinary, unremarkable or disappointingly mediocre.

New Sentence: What should have been a thrilling contest turned into a rather ho-hum affair.

August 5

New Word: Discomfiture = (N.) visibly uncomfortable and embarrased

New Sentence: I experienced considerable discomfiture when I realised I'd been confidently addressing the wrong person.

August 6

New Word: Inscrutable = (Adj.) impossible or extremely difficult to understand, interpret, or read.

New Sentence: The motives behind his decision remain inscrutable.

August 7

New Word: Reticent = (Adj.) reluctant to speak freely, reveal one's thoughts, or disclose information.

New Sentence: The company remained reticent about its plans for the division.

August 10

New Word: Indolent = (Adj.) habitually lazy; unwilling to make an effort or exert oneself.

New Sentence: His indolent habits frustrated colleagues who regarded him as immensely talented.

August 11

New Word: Verity = (N.) A fundamental or enduring truth.

New Sentence: The gritty verities of life.

August 12

New Word: Ineffable = (Adj.) too great, powerful, beautiful, strange, or profound to be adequately expressed in words.

New Sentence: There is an ineffable pathos in the final scene.

August 13

New Word: Schlep = (V.) to carry or drag something cumbersome with considerable effort, or to make a journey that feels long, inconvenient, or tiresome.

New Sentence: I carefully schlepped it home in one of my six glutted tote bags.

August 14

New Word: Mollified = (V.) to calm someone who is angry or upset, or to reduce their anger or anxiety.

New Sentence: The concessions failed to mollify the protesters.

August 17

New Word: bevy = (N.) a large or impressive group/collection of people or things. (Often used positively)

New Sentence: A bevy of cultural sacraments mark our passage into adulthood, but the yawning decades beyond it unspool without ceremony.

August 18

New Word: sacraments = (N.) a sacred, religious rite. Here, rituals or ceremonies that society treats as especially significant or almost sacred.

New Sentence: A bevy of cultural sacraments mark our passage into adulthood, but the yawning decades beyond it unspool without ceremony.

August 19

New Word: unspool = (V.) to unwind something from a spool or reel.

New Sentence: A bevy of cultural sacraments mark our passage into adulthood, but the yawning decades beyond it unspool without ceremony.

August 20

New Word: Sylphlike = (Adj.) slender, graceful, delicate and light in appearance or movement.

New Sentence: She moved with a sylphlike grace across the ballroom.

August 21

New Word: Hallowed = (Adj.) Greatly revered, deeply respected, or considered sacred. Could be used instead of 'holy'

New Sentence: They walked onto the hallowed turf of Wembley.

August 24

New Word: red herring = (N.) something that distracts attention from the real issue or deliberately leads people in the wrong direction.

New Sentence: The detective realised the missing necklace was a red herring.

August 25

New Word: Cauldron = (N.) Figuratively (very common): a place or situation of intense activity, conflict, emotion, or turmoil.

New Sentence: The stadium became a cauldron of noise as the home side equalised.

August 26

New Word: Tramps = (N.) Not only those who are homeless, but also who are wanderers. Impoverished wanderers.

New Sentence: A celebrity president who is a populist demagogue.

August 27

New Word: Gobsmacked = (Adj.) completely astonished, amazed, or stunned, usually because something is unexpected. (in conversation, not used in writing)

New Sentence: I was absolutely gobsmacked when I heard the news.

August 28

New Word: Wallowed = (V.) to indulge excessively in a particular emotion, situation, or state, especially a negative one. Or literally, lie and roll in the mud.

New Sentence: The country cannot afford to wallow in nostalgia.

August 31

New Word: Steeped = (Adj./V.) deeply immersed in, strongly influenced by, or thoroughly saturated with something.

New Sentence: The town is steeped in history.
