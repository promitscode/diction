Monthly Archive

October 2026

A continuing archive of words, meanings, and sentences gathered through October.

October 1

New Word: Petulant = (Adj.) Irritable, sulky, or bad-tempered in a childish way, especially when annoyed at not getting what one wants. It is quite a critical word.

New Sentence: He gave a petulant response when reporters pressed him on the failed negotiations.

October 2

New Word: Imprimatur = (N.) Official approval, endorsement, or sanction from an authority or influential person.

New Sentence: Her appointment gave the initiative an imprimatur of academic credibility.

October 5

New Word: Desultory = (Adj.) Lacking a clear plan, purpose, or sustained effort; proceeding in a disconnected, irregular, or half-hearted way.

New Sentence: Their conversation became increasingly desultory, wandering from politics to food to old school friends.

October 6

New Word: Missive = (N.) A letter or written message, especially one that is somewhat formal, lengthy, or important.

New Sentence: The company sent shareholders a detailed missive explaining the restructuring plan.

October 7

New Word: Posited = (V.) Put forward an idea, explanation, or assumption as a basis for argument, discussion, or further reasoning.

New Sentence: Economists have posited several explanations for the decline in productivity growth.

October 8

New Word: Mewls = (V.) Makes a weak, whimpering, crying sound, especially like that of a baby or a small animal.

New Sentence: He mewls about every minor inconvenience as though it were a catastrophe.

October 9

New Word: Lark = (N and V.) a playful adventure, amusing escapade, or activity undertaken for fun rather than serious purpose, or 'lark about'

New Sentence: What began as a weekend lark eventually grew into a successful business.

October 12

New Word: Slovenly = (Adj.) Untidy, careless, or dirty in appearance or habits; by extension, done in a careless, poorly organised, or imprecise way.

New Sentence: He arrived looking slovenly, with an unironed shirt and scuffed shoes.

October 13

New Word: Plaintive = (Adj.) sounding sad, mournful or wistful, often in a way that quietly expresses longing, sorrow or an appeal for sympathy.

New Sentence: The film is an elegy for a dying rural community. Its most poignant moment comes when an old farmer leaves his home for the last time. His daughter's plaintive goodbye gives the scene its extraordinary pathos.

October 14

New Word: Nondescript = (Adj.) Lacking distinctive, interesting, or memorable features; ordinary-looking and difficult to describe precisely.

New Sentence: He has a fairly nondescript appearance, with features that suggest South Asian ancestry.

October 15

New Word: Wicker = (N. & Adj) A material made by weaving flexible plant stems, reeds, cane, rattan, or similar fibres, commonly used to make baskets, chairs, tables, and other furniture.

New Sentence: The café was furnished with wicker tables and pale linen cushions.

October 16

New Word: Cave Canem = (x.) latin for beware of dog

New Sentence: His colleagues joked that the office door ought to carry a “cave canem” sign whenever he was in a foul mood.

October 19

New Word: xxxxx = (N.) A filmmaker—usually a director—whose films bear such a distinctive personal vision and style that they can be regarded as the “author” of those films.

New Sentence: The director has emerged as one of contemporary cinema's most distinctive auteurs.

October 20

New Word: Import = (N.) importance, significance, or weight. Can be used in place of 'importance'

New Sentence: Ideas gained import simply because he had articulated them.

October 21

New Word: Desultory = (Adj.) Lacking a clear plan, purpose, or consistency; moving from one thing to another in a disconnected or half-hearted way.

New Sentence: After dinner, they fell into a desultory conversation about books, politics and old friends.

October 22

New Word: Philistinism = (N.) A lack of interest in, understanding of, or appreciation for art, culture, literature, or intellectual pursuits.

New Sentence: His contempt for poetry and painting was less ignorance than a kind of deliberate philistinism.

October 23

New Word: Smorgasbord = (N.) Originally, a Swedish buffet offering a wide variety of dishes. Figuratively, it means a large and varied assortment of things. It is more vivid than array or range, but also less restrained.

New Sentence: The festival offered a smorgasbord of films from more than 40 countries.

October 26

New Word: Polemical = (Adj.) Usually suggests writing or speech that is combative. It is not merely “critical”; it implies a willingness to argue forcefully and often to provoke disagreement.

New Sentence: The speech was more polemical than conciliatory, leaving little room for compromise.

October 27

New Word: Disparaging = (Adj.) Disparaging is stronger than merely critical. It suggests that the speaker is putting someone or something down, often with contempt, scorn, or condescension.

New Sentence: He made disparaging remarks about the company’s competitors.

October 28

New Word: Wallflower = (N.) A shy or socially reserved person who tends to remain at the edge of social gatherings rather than actively joining in.

New Sentence: She began the evening as a wallflower but soon found herself chatting easily with strangers.

October 29

New Word: Squeamish = (Adj.) Easily made to feel sick, uneasy, embarrassed, or disturbed, especially by blood, injury, unpleasant bodily details, or morally uncomfortable subjects.

New Sentence: The documentary is not for the squeamish; several sequences are graphic and unsettling.

October 30

New Word: Impresario = (N.) A person who organises, manages, or promotes concerts, theatrical productions, festivals, or other forms of entertainment.

New Sentence: The festival’s impresario was as famous for his flamboyant personality as for the artists he promoted.
